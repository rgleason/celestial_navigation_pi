6. Input Lunar Sights into Celestial Navigation Plugin

Input the Measurements

For Lunars, there are 3 angle measurements to input. The angle between the Moon and the Celestial Body, the

Altitude of the Moon (w/Limb) to the Horizon. The Altitude of the Body (only the Sun has a Limb) to the Horizon.

Type: select Lunar.

Sight Properties Form. Celestial Body: select Sun

Also available are Planets, •

57 Navigation Stars and Polaris. 9 Stars known as Lunar Stars (see •

page 14 for more about a good selection).

Select Limb: select Near or Far

Refer to the next illustration for examples of Near or Far. •

The body is Near when the closest edge of a celestial body (Sun, •

Star, Planet) is brought to the nearest visible edge of the moon.

The body is Far when bringing the nearest edge of the body •

across the moon’s face to the far side of the moon.

Measurement. Input Lunar Distance Angle between the Moon and the

Celestial Body.

New Feature (v 2.4.66) - Sextant Measurements can be input in any of 3

OpenCPN supported “angles”. Decimal Degrees, Degrees-Min, and

Degrees-Min-Sec. Redisplayed per OpenCPN display preferences

Certainty or Altitude uncertainty (new. v 2.8.x). Measurement

accuracy. Used to predict position uncertainty. You’ll achieve better

certainty with Lunar distance than Altitude Measurements because

Lunar distance doesn’t have Dip which is a large source of inaccuracy,
