Choose a lunar pair

9. Which Celestial Bodies paired with the Moon make a great Lunar?

Following cited by experienced Lunar Navigators. No hard and fast rule to pick the best one.

#1

Sun and 50% illuminated “Half Moon”

  • The most frequent Lunars pair. The boundary between Sun and Moon is exceedingly sharp/high contrast which is a big advantage for accuracy. It doesn’t literally have to be half Moon. The example was 25% illum. Use judgement.
  • A 1st or 3rd quarter Moon can calculate time. Position (2.8x solution) might be weak because the 2 body fix doesn’t have a 60-120° spread typical of valid 2 body fixes. The spread is better when using a 50% illuminated Moon.

#2

Planets (Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn) and Moon

  • Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn travel along the Ecliptic plane. They make for a good pair with the Moon.
  • In the Evening and through Twilight Morning and Evening, there is almost always at least 1 visible major planet.

#3 Bright Stars near the Ecliptic Plane with the Moon. Sharp!

  • There were 9 Lunar bright stars known as “Lunar Stars” Bright, close to the Ecliptic Plane, spread 360° East to West, and North to South. Lunar Distances were pre-calculated in the Nautical Almanac and Bowditch American Practical Navigator. There are a few others. Scored lower in the Lunar criteria. Vega and Deneb isn’t on the list. That’s because they aren’t remotely close to the Ecliptic. They’d be poor selections compared to the “9” to pair with moon to tell TIME. But perfectly fine to measure distance.
