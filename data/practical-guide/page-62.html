Reduce measurement errors

11. Tips to improve Lunar Accuracy

  • A high quality metal sextant with good inspection report. No arc errors throughout the angle range you’ll measure.
  • The higher the magnification, the better. 7X is readily available.
  • Well Adjusted. Perpendicularity, then side and index error. Goal is as close to 0.0 as possible. These errors impact Lunar accuracy.
  • The very best Lunar Distance observation. Altitude sight accuracy is not as important for UTC Time.
  • Practice-Practice-Practice. Ship Navigators took sights almost daily. That’s why they are so good.
  • Select a celestial body that is traveling along the same trajectory plane as the Moon.
  • Start off with Sun, it gives the best result.
  • Then try Planets because they travel close to the Moon’s trajectory.
  • Then “Lunar Stars”. They were bright and close to the ecliptic (some closer than others).
  • Don’t select a celestial body just because it is near the Moon. There are often better candidates.
  • Consider a body who’s LD angle is between 60° to 100°. 90° was considered optimal with the Sun. Smaller than 60° is easier. Pre-set Sextant make larger angles possible. Sometimes, larger LDo fixes problems when the body isn’t on the Moon travel path. Larger than 100° measurements are harder to do, but possible. Did your sextant inspection report include angles > 90°? Cel Nav Plugin has a Collimation form in Lunar Tools to assess your Sextant’s collimation.
  • Don’t select a star that is far off the moon’s path or far off the ecliptic (e.g. Vega) and expect good Time results.
  • Ensure the sharpest focus and high contrast. You want to see a clear outline of the Moon and body.
  • Hold the sextant frame with both hands to avoid wobbling. Do small and few micrometer adjustments. Presetting helps
  • Make sure the body is aligned (an imaginary line) to the center of the Field of View. That way, the sextant frame is aligned to the plane of the bodies. You’ll be rocking your sextant to ensure the touch is tangent.
  • Preset the sextant to the expected Lunar Distance. Then wait for the body to touch the Moon.
  • In the Lunars chapter of Bowditch’s American Practical Navigator, the editors recommended that the center of the body (planet, star) touches the moon. This indicates that the navigators used sextants with good magnification.
  • Try not to do “live” adjustments to the micrometer. Pre-set sextant to the predicted LD, then patiently wait for the “touch” to occur, and then MARK. Then turn the micrometer a little ahead by the ∆ of LD arc-min/min of time and wait for the next MARK. RMB 26 Sept, 2026
