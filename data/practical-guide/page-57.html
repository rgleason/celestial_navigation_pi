Improve lunar observations

11. Tips to improve Lunar sights Accuracy?

  • A high quality Sextant. Collimated through the entire index arm arc-range. Minimize all adjustable Sextant Errors. They should be as close to 0 as possible. If you don’t know it, check collimation using the Lunar Tools form (new 2.8.x).
  • Your initial goal is to consistently achieve better than +/- 0.3’ LDo measurement accuracy.
  • Experienced Navigators have achieve between +/- 0.1’ to +/- 0.2 LDo measurement accuracy. 0.1 is achieved by averaging Lunar Sights. The sight error should be between 3’ to 6’ Longitude.

Easier to achieve LDo measurement accuracy than Altitude sight measurement accuracy. Because the LDo measurement has no DIP! The problem with DIP is the uncertainty in the Height of Eye due to your vessel bobbing up and down, timing the swells when sighting the horizon, and unpredictable nature of anomalous refraction.

None of this DIP uncertainty exists when measuring Lunar Distance LDo. There is no DIP!

  • You should shoot 2 Altitude Sights for Lunars. The purpose is to determine Parallax, Refraction, and Limb corrections. And for Lunars, these altitude sights don’t need to be “spot on”.

The 2 Altitude Sights can afterwards be used for a 2-body fix. Your Altitude Sight goal should initially be 1’ or less “sight error”. Sextant professionals consistently get no worse than 0.5’ sight error. That gives an idea for improvement potential. Dip correction uncertainty is the cause of some of the “Sight error”, and the error depends on ship size and stability. So there is judgement to what goal is achievable.

Combine that with a Polar sight during twilight, a Sun Sight (sun-run-sun), Meridian Transit, you’ll have a Running Fix with 3 bodies.

  • In the 1700’s, Celestial mechanics was good enough for the times, but not perfect. Now the Commercial Edition of the Nautical Almanac is accurate mostly 0.0’ error with occasional 0.1’ error. And U.S. Navy Observatory Celestial Data is better than that. In a 2024 study of the Celestial Nav Plugin Altitude sights versus Commercial Edition of the Nautical Almanac, the Plugin was +/- 0.1 (@1 standard deviation) of the Nautical Almanac. And at least 50% of the time, it matched the Almanac.
  • The Use Case 1 Lunar measurement was initially off by 0.6’. Room for improvement! Try the following tips.
