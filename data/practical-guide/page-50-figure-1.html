Longitude - Historically, Longitude was the objective (after TIME). In 2.8.x, Latitude and Longitude is derived using the Moon and the Body. The 46° spread of the 2 body fix is shown in description. A spread between 60° to 120° is a valid Fix. The narrow spread might still be in the ballpark for Longitude. The accuracy of Lunars with the best Sextant and perfect to near perfect measurement is roughly 10-20 seconds, maybe 3nm. Historically, Navigators used a meridian transit to determine an accurate Latitude but not Longitude. Because the Sun’s “hang time” at noon, made Longitude not accurate. They trusted their DR skills between the Meridian Transit Sight and the Lunar Sight. And if the Sun-Moon Lunar was a relatively short time from the Meridian Transit, They determined Longitude based on an assumed Latitude which they maintained by DR. Today, when we do Lunars, Lunar experts stop at UTC time because we have additional capabilities. But from the 18th to mid 19th century, Longitude was the reason for doing Lunars. Distance from the DR - 16.1 nm. Position is calculated from DR position (La, Lo) to 2 body fix position (La, Lo). Not a good Lunar measurement! Lunars are most sensitive to LD measurement. This bad sight was used to illustrate Lunar Distance Measurement Error needs to be minimized. Good news is that the Moon and Sun outlines are incredibly clear. With a lot of practice and reasonable ship stability, you should achieve 0.2 arc-min Lunar Distance measurement error. With averaging, maybe 0.1’
