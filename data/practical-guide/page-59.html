11. Tips to improve Lunar Accuracy (continued)

  • Bowdich chapter on Lunars has a lot of practical advice.
  • Average the run of Sights. Or Plot the sights taken and choose an angle measurement/time pair on the best fit line How to Choose the Sight data from a Run of sights using a Plot of sights.
  • Slide 5 shows how to shoot a run of sights for Lunars.
  • And calculate Average Time and Average Measurement for the 3 groups: Lunar Sight, Moon Sight, and Celestial Body Sight. And then you’d input the average data into the Celestial Nav plugin forms. But, what if the Average time isn’t roughly the same?
  • The alternative is to plot the individual Sight data and fit 3 lines: 1 line through the Lunar sights, 1 line through the Moon sights, and 1 line through the Celestial Body sight. It is best done on 1 plotting sheet.
  • The Altitude / Lunar Measurements the y-axis. The elapsed minutes the x-Axis.
  • Visually draw 3 lines to fit the 3 sets of data. Or use a spreadsheet to fit it for you.
  • Visually, select somewhere along the X-axis and draw a vertical line from that point.
  • Where vertical line intersects the 3 plotted lines, is the measurement data for x-axis UTC Time. How to pre-setup your sextant index to make it easier to do a Lunar Sight?
  • It is sometimes difficult to bring the Celestial Body across to the Moon. A simpler approach is to predict the LD angle for a given time and Lunar Pair, and set your sextant to that LD angle.
  • Then, sight the Moon. With a little rotation of your sextant, you’ll see the Celestial object near the moon.
