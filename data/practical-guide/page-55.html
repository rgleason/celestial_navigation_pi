9. Which Celestial Bodies paired with the Moon make a great Lunar?

#4

Selection and what LD measurement to target

  • Outdoors, pick a bright sharp star/planet on the Moon’s trajectory/path, “Ecliptic". They are often among the best Lunar pair.
  • If Lunar Distance is too small, the Lunar Distance can be “Cleared” if done carefully and time might be ok. But, Position (Lat or Lon) might not be so good depending on how narrow the spread is between the two bodies.
  • If the Lunar Distance hourly change rate is very small, then LD Cleared can be good, but time will be less accurate.
  • If large (i.e. > 100°), it gets harder to sight the body with the moon. Pre-set the Sextant and practice helps.
  • A good LD Range is 60° - 100°. Historically, a 90° spread when doing Half Moon-Sun Lunar was considered ideal.
  • Moon or Celestial Body Altitude higher than horizon (>5° to 10°) to avoid anomalous atmospheric refraction.

#5

Full or almost Full Moon and a Star. Does Moon’s glow wash out dim stars or make the touchpoint unsharp or unclear? Measuring Far Edge of the Sun to the Near Edge of the Moon is not going to give you good results compared to Near Edge of the Sun to the Near Edge of the Moon. The clearer and more contrast for the boundary layer, the better the measurement. That’s why the standard Lunar Practice is Near Edge of the Moon to the Near Edge of the Sun.

There is no universally accepted rule. Selection depends on your objective: LD

Clear accuracy, Time accuracy, Latitude/Longitude. Use your “Seaman eyes”! Try it and see what works for you.
