Planner follow-up for 2.9.3 -- issue #354
=======================================

Review: https://github.com/rgleason/celestial_navigation_pi/issues/354

The header wraps complete related groups, keeping latitude/longitude and
event display/zone offset together. Hour-step buttons advance the entire
sky by an elapsed UTC hour and propagate a moving observer's position.
UTC and fixed-zone entry no longer passes through a possibly nonexistent
computer-local hour at a daylight-saving transition. Native date and clock
pickers are independent, preserving the same instant when presentation changes.

Sky names use a remembered Sparse/Balanced/More choice independent of the
magnitude filter. Balanced is the default. Brighter bodies and the selected
body take priority; alternative placements and short leaders avoid names and
markers. Hover identifies an unnamed dot. A selected above-horizon body stays
on the plot even outside the magnitude filter. SHA/declination includes the
polar regions. Moon motion is shown relative to stars only on SHA/declination;
the misleading time-varying trail against a fixed local sky is removed.

Sight preference separates visibility, sextant handling and fix geometry.
The preference weights visibility 75% and altitude handling 25%, keeping a
bright high star useful with a preset sextant. Twilight penalises faint stars
more strongly. Guidance replaces the unexplained observability number;
brightness values for planets/Sun/Moon are representative, not current
photometry. Geometry is unchanged. Three-body suggestions precede pairs;
Polaris remains in the body list and latitude helper and can optionally be
included in fixes. Daylight stars are excluded from recommended fixes.

Both lunar planners offer Traditional lunar companions (default) or All
calculated companions. Traditional means Sun, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn,
Hamal, Aldebaran, Pollux, Regulus, Spica, Antares, Altair, Fomalhaut and Markab.
The historical nine-star list is also given in Bob's Practical Guide and
the Nautical Almanac tables reproduced at https://reednavigation.com/lunars/na.html.
Magnitude is visible in both tables. Practical order prefers fewer cautions,
then brighter companions, then timing; timing-first remains available.
Ecliptic latitude is context, without an extra penalty on the actual distance
rate. The full body list and numerical lunar calculations remain available.

The HTML, editable DOCX and bundled PDF manual describe these controls and
the Compact policy already committed for 2.9.3. The cover now states 2.9.3.

Validation evidence: validation/planner-v2.9.3. Eight CTest groups pass,
including numerical audits in four time zones; 16 focused cases pass.
The native temporary-profile UI check covers wide/narrow headers, all planning
modes and label densities, lunar columns, overlay availability, UTC stepping
and both time-entry formats across the London spring DST gap. Manual validation
checks offline images, abbreviations, glossary and PDF; all 44 rendered pages
were visually reviewed. Package data hashes, runtime licences and the stripped
production library were checked before installation.

These preferences are transparent planning heuristics, not visibility
probabilities or accuracy estimates. Four-body recommendations are deferred.
Linux desktop checks do not replace Windows/macOS/Android qualification.
