The negative control deliberately selects the old spherical model at Point
Judith. Its Moon altitude differs from Horizons by 6.1463″, so the reference
test demonstrably distinguishes that approximation from the WGS84 result.

## Point Judith observations

These are results from the unchanged engine, **not independent truths**:

* Published averaged-input example, branch near the DR: correction −8.457 s;
  formal time sigma 26.289 s. The lab uses a ±60 s scan; small differences from
  earlier broad-scan results are within the 0.05 s root tolerance.
* Eight original timed readings, joint position/time fit: correction −8.633 s;
  formal time sigma 13.142 s; angular RMS residual 0.136′. This fit uses an
  explicit stationary-observer assumption and assumed 0.2′ reading sigmas.
* Conditioning the same watch on the published DR produces −0.431 s and formal
  time sigma 1.064 s. **Do not advertise this as one-second lunar clock
  accuracy:** fixing position gives the timed altitude readings strong clock
  information and ignores uncertainty in the DR. It is a different problem.
* Shared observations contribute eight residuals, not 24. Appending another
  copy of an already-used triple leaves the fitted correction and uncertainty
  unchanged.

The original Sun pair averages to 51°58.5′, whereas the published averaged
example uses 51°58′. Both remain exactly as sourced. The incomplete Linnman
artificial-horizon example remains intake-only, with its missing metadata
recorded; it contributes no accuracy pass/fail assertions.

## Interpretation and next step

The baseline is now reproducible and the experimental harness has an initial
equivalence result. The next controlled numerical experiment should address
dated Earth-orientation inputs in the candidate alone, retaining these results
and tolerances. Broader lunar observations and independent limb/refraction
benchmarks are still needed. No release version, working OpenCPN installation,
